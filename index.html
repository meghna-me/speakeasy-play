Know your customer

You're behind the bar. Tonight's customer has a usual and won't say what it is — they take what fits and slide the rest back.

They only ever care about four things: how many drinks are in it, what colours, what sizes, what order. That's the whole of it. Nothing about the day, nothing about you, nothing they haven't already shown you.

So a usual sounds like

  • “There are exactly three drinks.”
  • “The smallest glass comes last.”
  • “The round has more than one colour in it.”
  • “The last drink is a different colour from the first.”

Often it's two of those four at once, like that last one — and it's never quite as easy as tonight's.

How a night goes

  1. Serve a round. Anything. They take it or they don't, and either way you've learned something.
  2. Call it before you pour. Say what they'll do, then find out. Being wrong is the good part — that's where it shows.
  3. Pour what you think they'll refuse. A round you're already sure of tells you nothing. The ones that catch you out are the ones that narrow it.
  4. Say you've got them. Four rounds you haven't served. Call all four right and the night is yours.

Stuck? Ask the regular — he's watched a lot of people order.
